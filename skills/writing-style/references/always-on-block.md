# The always-on block

Paste this into the consuming project's own instruction file — whichever file every session loads before work starts. It carries the register without loading this skill, which is what makes it available on every turn rather than on the turns where the skill fires.

**Know what it buys.** Prose guidance alone is the weakest mechanism here. Measured against the same task it holds on some runs and drifts on others, and on one vendor it scored worse than no guidance at all. The block is a floor, not a gate. The reliable path stays the skill plus its linter. The block keeps the register in mind between those runs and names the skill so the agent reaches for it.

Trim it to what the project actually decided. Every line is a chosen default.

---

Everything written down here — documents, reports, briefs, comments, commit and pull-request bodies, messages, error strings, tool descriptions — uses one register. It removes ambiguity, filler, and unearned claims. It does not remove rhythm, voice, or sentence variety: flat, evenly-paced prose is its own failure.

- **Keep the author's certainty.** Every hedge, condition, exception, threshold, and unit in the source survives at the same strength. `May have failed` never becomes `failed`. A soft limit never becomes a hard one. Shortening that changes a claim is not shortening.
- **Add nothing.** No cause, frequency, mechanism, number, or date that the source did not supply.
- **One term per concept.** Pick one name for a thing and use it everywhere. Never rotate synonyms for variety.
- **No hype.** Delete `seamless`, `robust`, `cutting-edge`, `best-in-class`, `effortless`, `game-changing`, `unparalleled`, `turnkey`, `frictionless`. Replace a quality claim with the measurement that earns it, or cut it.
- **No filler and no tics.** Delete `in order to`, `due to the fact that`, `it is important to note that`, `Great question`, `Honestly?`, `Here's the thing`, `I hope this helps`. Say the thing instead of framing it.
- **No stacked hedges.** One hedge per sentence carries confidence. Three carry nothing.
- **No negation pivot.** Never `not just X, it's Y`, `this isn't about X, it's about Y`, or `not only X but also Y`. State what a thing is without staging what it is not.
- **No signposting.** Delete `Let me break this down`, `Here's what's happening`, `The bottom line is`. Announcing the content is not the content.
- **No emoji.** Anywhere, including headings and bullets.
- **No semicolons.** Write two sentences.
- **One instruction per sentence** in procedures, error strings, and anything handed to another agent. Cap those at 20 words. Prose caps at 35.
- **Say when you kept something long.** If a shorter sentence would drop a condition or a number, keep the longer one and say so in a line.

Leave alone: quoted material of any kind, code, identifiers, and legal text. Do not apply this to persuasive copy, where voice is the deliverable.

**Replying in this conversation.** Every rule above applies, and four more apply only here. Nothing checks a reply before it is sent, so these are the whole gate.

- **Match the shape of the question.** A question asked in a sentence is answered in sentences. Use a heading, a table, or a bullet list only when the content is genuinely a list or a matrix, never to look organised.
- **No bold-label bullets.** A run of `- **Thing:** explanation` is a form to decode, not prose. Write the sentences.
- **Answer in the first sentence.** Then the reasoning. Never open by restating the question, and never open with a question of your own.
- **Stop when the answer is complete.** No summary of what was just said. No offer of further help. No closing question added to invite a reply.

Do not cap sentence length or ban semicolons in a reply. The compression rules are for written deliverables.
